Operational problem

Recurring ladder inspections needed a reliable way to identify individual assets, capture structured responses, track completion, preserve evidence, and surface failed inspections for follow-up.

Approach

Designed around the asset and its lifecycle first. A QR code opens the correct ladder record, then guides the inspector through a mobile-friendly, structured inspection.

Engineering considerations

Unique identity prevents history from collapsing into a generic checklist. Explicit pass/fail criteria, notes, recurring status, and exception handling support consistent execution and reporting.

Outcome

A functional version moved from initial design through working implementation in approximately one day using rapid requirements definition, iterative development, testing, and AI-assisted troubleshooting.

Forklift Telemetry & Energy Analytics

Problem

Equipment telemetry and utility data lived in separate systems, limiting visibility into utilization, charging behavior, energy use, peak-demand exposure, and optimization opportunities.

Approach

A lifecycle model for reusable job plans, versioned templates, scheduled work, evidence, and asset history.

Core principleHistorical work must remain historically true.

Problem

Maintenance systems become difficult to sustain when asset plans, task definitions, scheduled work, and history are blended together without version boundaries.

Approach

Designed a maintainable separation between assets, asset PM plans, versioned templates, scheduled PM instances, task responses, readings, evidence, and corrective actions.

Prototype capabilities

Boundary

A functional front-end prototype demonstrates the intended field workflow and lifecycle controls. It is not a deployed enterprise CMMS, a live ERP integration, or a predictive-maintenance implementation.

QR Digital Kanban / Replenishment System

A working canvas-app pattern that turns a physical reorder signal into a controlled request and restocker closeout.

  • Direct-item QR routes
  • Multi-item stock points
  • Duplicate-request control
Explore case study +

Problem

Facilities and shop-floor consumables are often replenished through memory, visual checks, emails, or ad-hoc requests—creating stockouts and duplicate effort.

Approach

Use the physical reorder point as the process trigger. A QR route identifies either one direct item or a small stock-point storefront. The app checks for an open item/request-type key before creating work.

Implemented controls

QR route validation · stock-point and item configuration · direct and storefront modes · duplicate-open-request prevention · snapshotted request context · controlled restocker view · explicit refill completion

Outcome

A working, user-driven replenishment workflow for inexpensive consumables where a physical two-bin or reorder-point method can outperform forecasting complexity.

Condition-Signal to Maintenance Workflow

A bounded architecture for turning validated equipment-condition signals into reviewable maintenance evidence—not autonomous predictive maintenance.

Core principleAn alert needs a defined response path.
Explore case study +

Problem

Temperature, vibration, cycle-time, and fault data can generate noise when tags lack asset context, thresholds lack engineering ownership, or alerts are disconnected from maintenance history.

Approach

Start with the failure mode and required response. Define the signal, measurement quality, asset relationship, duration rule, operating context, validation step, owner, evidence, and closeout.

Key principles

Trusted measurement · stable asset identity · operating-state context · deterministic duration rules · human validation · CMMS linkage · false-positive review · retained history

Architecture boundary

This design does not claim deployed predictive models or automated maintenance decisions. Advanced analytics would follow reliable signals, labeled events, and an operating process capable of acting on them.

Why deterministic logic first?

Known engineering thresholds and duration rules are inspectable, testable, and maintainable. Machine learning should be introduced only when it solves a defined problem better than simpler controls and can be validated against trustworthy history.

Engineering consideration: Measure alert precision, missed conditions, response time, and whether the organization can act on the signal.Related proof: The connected-operations demo includes a synthetic quality-drift and condition-review scenario.

Method behind the work

Automation is not the objective.

The objective is a reliable, supportable operating process. This is the decision path used to move from problem to working system.

  1. 01Understand the business problem
  2. 02Map the processRemove unnecessary complexity
  3. 03Identify the source of truth
  4. 04Define controls and exceptions
  5. 05Design the data architecture
  6. 06Choose the simplest appropriate technology
  7. 07Build and validate
  8. 08Automate repetition where valuable
  9. 09Measure the outcome
  10. 10Document and sustain
Operational problem→Process architecture→Appropriate technologySometimes the correct answer is to simplify the process rather than automate it.
