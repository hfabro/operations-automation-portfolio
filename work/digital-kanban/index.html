QR Digital Kanban & Replenishment

A low-friction digital replenishment signal based on a physical reorder point—designed to preserve the simplicity of Kanban while adding duplicate prevention, order visibility, fulfillment state, and history.

Operational problem

Do not make a simple signal complicated.

Facilities and shop-floor consumables are often replenished through visual checks, memory, email, or ad-hoc requests. The process is easy to start but hard to see: users do not know whether an item is already ordered, buyers receive duplicates, and fulfillment history disappears.

Constraint

The user should do almost nothing

At the reorder point, the user should identify the stock location and confirm the signal. Complex item search, quantity decisions, forecasting, and administrative fields would weaken the process.

Approach

Bind the QR to the stock point

The scan supplies item/location identity and standard replenishment logic. The system checks for an open request before creating another and makes status visible to the next user.

CommoditiesConsumables with practical physical thresholds
User actionScan and confirm
Back-office actionOrder, fulfill, close, preserve history

Architecture

Let the physical system trigger the digital one.

Supply locationKnown item and physical reorder point
QR scanStock point identity and context
Request controlCheck for one existing open signal
Purchasing / fulfillmentOrder, receive, replenish, update state
Close & historyCompletion, timing, exception, audit trail
Threshold choice

Not every item needs forecasting

For low-cost, stable, visually consumed items, a well-designed physical reorder point may be more reliable and maintainable than a forecast-heavy system.

System choice

Digitize visibility, not waste

The value is the bridge between physical demand and owned fulfillment—not adding data entry or automation for its own sake.

Controls & failure modes

One signal, visible through closeout.

Request controls

Prevent noise

  • QR resolves a unique stock point
  • Standard replenishment quantity is pre-defined
  • Existing open request blocks duplicates
  • User sees already-ordered status immediately
  • Different threshold types remain configurable
Fulfillment controls

Make work visible

  • Purchasing and fulfillment status are explicit
  • Owner and timestamps support follow-up
  • Exceptions preserve shortages or substitutions
  • Closeout returns the stock point to ready state
  • History supports lead-time and reliability analysis

Intended outcome

A replenishment signal people will actually use.

The architecture supports janitorial supplies, break-room consumables, shop towels, cups, disposable utensils, bagged ice, and similar facility consumables where a physical reorder threshold and standard quantity are practical.

Front lineMinimal interaction at the stock point
CoordinatorOwned queue and visible fulfillment
SystemDuplicate control and auditable history

Design rationale

Simplicity is an architecture decision.

  1. A physical signal can outperform a complex forecast when item economics and consumption behavior support it.
  2. Duplicate prevention should happen before a request is created, not after a buyer receives it.
  3. Status visibility closes the trust gap between the person who signals need and the person who fulfills it.
