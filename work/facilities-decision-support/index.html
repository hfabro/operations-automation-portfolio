Compare operating choices using evidence, assumptions, lifecycle costs, and execution gates—while keeping program calculations, modeled opportunities, and realized outcomes distinct.

Operational problem

A useful business case makes uncertainty visible.

A facilities portfolio can contain completed work, calculations, estimates, proposals, and alternatives at the same time. Treating them as one savings total can double count an opportunity or convert a proposed change into an apparent result.

Energy

Distinguish calculation from measurement

In a fictional compressed-air scenario, a repair that avoids an average 36 kW for 7,000 hours/year would avoid 252,000 kWh/year. At an assumed $0.14/kWh avoidable energy rate, that is $35,280/year gross before repair and other operating costs. It is conditional, not a measured or achieved employer result.

Capital

Compare the complete lifecycle

A repair, rental, or replacement decision needs scope, criticality, redundancy, service impact, operating cost, maintenance, outage risk, useful life, and execution evidence—not purchase price alone.

Utility analysis boundary

Align interval data and identify normalization assumptions before comparing periods. A normalized cost is not the actual billed cost, and a year-over-year trend does not by itself establish project-level savings.

Controls & failure modes

Keep assumptions and overlap visible.

Evidence controls

Keep claims in their lane

  • Record whether a value is measured, program-calculated, modeled, or a quote
  • Separate an approval decision from completed execution
  • Flag missing assumptions and stale inputs
  • Keep unapproved financial figures out of the public portfolio
Portfolio controls

Prevent false totals

  • Identify overlapping or mutually exclusive opportunities
  • Do not add a component twice to a portfolio total
  • Separate service quality and risk decisions from savings
  • Reconcile actual invoices or meters before claiming realization

Design lessons

What makes the approach supportable.

  1. An assumption is not an outcome, even when a workbook calculates it precisely.
  2. Alternatives and overlapping energy projects must be reconciled before aggregation.
  3. The decision can have reliability or service value without a defensible savings claim.
