Facilities & Compliance Automation

A governed Microsoft 365 operating system for recurring requirements, individual task occurrences, ownership, due dates, evidence, reminders, exceptions, audit history, and management visibility.

Operational problem

Calendars and reminders are not a control system.

Recurring facilities and compliance obligations can fragment across spreadsheets, email, calendars, individual memory, and document repositories. The risk is not only missed work—it is uncertainty about ownership, evidence, status, and what happened when.

Constraint

One requirement, many occurrences

The governing requirement changes slowly, while each scheduled occurrence has its own due date, owner, status, evidence, exceptions, and history.

Approach

Separate definition from execution

A requirement master controls recurrence and accountability; automation generates discrete occurrences that can be worked, evidenced, escalated, completed, and reported.

Source of truthRequirement master
Execution recordScheduled occurrence
GovernanceOwner, evidence, exception, audit history

Architecture

The occurrence is the auditable unit of work.

Requirement masterScope, frequency, owner, control definition
Scheduled occurrenceUnique due date and execution record
Owner / statusAssignment, lifecycle, and visibility
NotificationsReminder and exception routing
Evidence & reportingCompletion record, history, management view
  1. Do not overwrite the requirement.
    The master defines what should recur; it should not become the record of every execution.
  2. Give every occurrence an identity.
    A discrete task record enables ownership, evidence, due-date control, exception handling, and defensible history.
  3. Automate generation, not accountability.
    Scheduled creation and reminders reduce administrative repetition, while named owners still make decisions and resolve exceptions.

Controls & failure modes

Make lateness, ambiguity, and missing evidence visible.

Lifecycle controls

Consistent execution

  • Controlled recurrence and occurrence generation
  • Explicit owner and due date
  • Defined status transitions
  • Completion requires appropriate evidence
  • Requirement changes do not erase prior work
Exception controls

Escalate what needs attention

  • Overdue and at-risk work remains visible
  • Missing or invalid evidence is not silent
  • Exceptions preserve reason, owner, and resolution
  • Duplicate occurrence generation is prevented
  • Management reporting traces back to the record

Outcome

A recurring obligation becomes a managed lifecycle.

The platform structures 143 active requirements and more than 500 recurring annual tasks into a system where ownership, due dates, status, evidence, reminders, exceptions, audit history, and management visibility can be governed together.

Scale143 active requirements
Throughput500+ annual occurrences
ValueReduced fragmentation and stronger visibility

What I learned

The data model is the operating model.

  1. Requirement and occurrence separation is the foundation for recurrence, evidence, history, and reporting.
  2. A reminder has little value without a named owner, a controlled status, an exception path, and an auditable closeout.
  3. Dashboards become trustworthy only after the underlying source of truth and lifecycle definitions are reliable.
