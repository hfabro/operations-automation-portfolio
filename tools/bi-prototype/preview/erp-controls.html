    How the ERP model is scoped.

    What the cost buckets mean

    A PO line has a site, spend category, account, project, asset, and work order. Cost-center distributions carry department and percentage shares. The pump purchase is split 70% Facilities / 30% Production. A project is an additional analytical dimension, not another copy of the purchase. Capital/operating labels describe this fictional scenario and are not accounting advice or evidence of capitalization policy.

    Matching, amounts, and limits

    All PO lines start approved. Whole-unit receipt/acceptance is required before invoice posting. Invoice quantity cannot exceed accepted uninvoiced quantity or the PO balance. A fixed, inclusive 1% unit-price tolerance is demonstrated. Supplier invoice references are checked across the current scenario, after trimming and case normalization. Exact cents and deterministic allocation preserve totals. Each invoice requires separate authorization before simulated payment.

    Open commitment = approved PO quantity less invoiced quantity, valued at the PO unit price. Receipt proxy = accepted quantity less invoiced quantity, valued at the PO price. Net invoice amounts may differ within tolerance. Tax, freight, discounts, FX, returns, credit notes, PO revisions/cancellations, non-PO expenses, retention, inventory valuation, and real GL posting are not implemented in this slice. Approved non-PO exceptions and credits belong in later explicit workflows; no PO is invented to hide their absence.

    Session-only demonstration; no records or funds leave the browser. Financial stages are teaching measures, not an ERP general ledger. Real system rules and account structures require configuration and verification for the chosen ERP.
