Four-step review

How to evaluate a demo.

01 / Scenario

Read the operational context

Start with the problem and constraints rather than judging the interface in isolation.

02 / Operate

Complete the workflow

Use the controls, change state, create an exception, and observe what information follows the transaction.

03 / Inspect

Look for the control model

Identify source of truth, identity, ownership, allowed transitions, validation, evidence, and audit history.

04 / Boundary

Review what is—and is not—claimed

Each demo states its synthetic scope and separates proof of design thinking from production deployment claims.

Shared design language

Different systems. Consistent discipline.

Operational

Work before software

  • Specific user, trigger, decision, and exception
  • Minimal interaction at the point of work
  • Visible ownership and completion criteria
  • Useful history for follow-up and learning
Technical

Supportability before novelty

  • Explicit identities and relationships
  • Deterministic rules where predictability matters
  • Bounded AI and human review
  • No hidden live services or external data dependencies

Safety & privacy

Synthetic by design.

These browser demonstrations use fictional assets, people, locations, identifiers, measures, and layouts. They do not connect to employer systems, SharePoint, Power Platform environments, OT networks, equipment, or production data. Controls shown in the browser are simulations only.

What the demos prove

Independent end-to-end ability to translate operational requirements into a coherent interaction, data, control, validation, and documentation model. Enterprise implementation would still require the appropriate Operations, IT, OT/Engineering, cybersecurity, data, safety, and change-control ownership.
